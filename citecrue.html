IX ULYSSE A MONTPARNASSE

Cités cruelles et suffocantes. Rues.
Les sueurs, perles noires, aux crins des passantes
Et les regards si lassés des grues
Dans leurs grottes de couloirs. Circéens. Pas

Après pas, fil après fil, vides -ô vide
Des avenues près de grands murs exilés-
Toiles suspendant sur les goudrons sans ride
Vos pièges pour les promeneurs désailés

Jusqu'aux bétons dressant leurs miroirs, falaises
Où vient battre la mer des chromes. Des coups
(Les chansons crachent leurs entrailles de braises
Mais les âniers ne sont maîtres des licous)

D'oiseaux grisés brisés contre les murs leurres
(Un franc ou deux - pour la musique) Couloirs
Ouvrant vos gueules de terreurs. Plaintes. Pleurent
Les chiens rouges tapis au sommeils des loirs.

Le vieux qui dort aux portes du cimetière
Montparnasse. Le cierge priape. Le croc.
Qu'y Gérard pende une indifférence altière.
Ulysse, sur la mer mauve, tant d'accrocs!

Michel Galiana (c) juillet-août 1990


IX ULYSSES IN MONTPARNASSE

1. Cruel and suffocating cities. Street mazes,
Black beads of sweat on the manes of the passers‑by,
And the whores’ so weary gazes
In their corridor‑ caves. Circean gazes. Step

2. After step, thread after thread, voids — Ovid, O void
Of these avenues flanking their great exiled walls —
Webs suspending over tar unrippled deployed
As traps for the walkers who have lost their wings’ call.

3. Up to the concrete cliffs proffering their mirrors
Where the ocean of chrome comes beating. Where shatter
Infuriating songs spitting ember-guts, terror
But donkey‑drivers never master their halters.

4.  Dazed birds break themselves against the huge lure‑walls
(A penny or two — for the music). Corridors
Opening wide jaws of terrors. Laments. Wails fall
From red dogs, crouched in dormice’s sleep-drawn floors.

5. And the old man asleep at the graveyard’s entrance —
Montparnasse. The priapic candle. The hook.
Where Gérard has hung his aloof indifference.
Ulysses, on the crimson sea, many snags, look!
Transl. Christian Souchon 01.01.2005 - 2026 (c) (r) All rights reserved

Note :

Ce monologue intérieur fait par son contenu (déambulation dans les rues d'une grande cité, combinée avec des allusions à l'Odyssée - Circé...) et sa forme (cheminement de la pensée consciente, parodie, jeux de mots...) explicitement référence à l'"Ulysse" de James Joyce.  Capstone Synthesis: The Triple Fusion
In Ulysse à Montparnasse, Michel superimposes three wandering traditions into a single urban hallucination.

- From Homer, he takes the mythic scaffolding: Circe’s enchantresses become Parisian grues, the corridors of Montparnasse turn into grottoes, and Ulysses himself reappears on a “mauve sea” of chrome and concrete.
- From Joyce, he borrows the syntax and sensory drift of Molly Bloom’s soliloquy — the crimson sea, the donkeys, the birds, the breathless free‑verse opening — transforming Montparnasse into a Joycean consciousness‑stream.
- From Nerval, he draws the nocturnal gravity of Paris: the priapic candle, the hook of Rue de la Vieille Lanterne, the aloof indifference of the poet who hanged himself there.

Michel binds these three worlds through wordplay (O vide/Ovide, mur leurre/hurleur, priape/prière) and through prosody: chaos in the first stanza, classical hendecasyllables thereafter. The result is a single wandering figure — Homeric, Joycean, Nervalian — crossing a modern Ithaca where myth, modernity, and melancholy converge.
