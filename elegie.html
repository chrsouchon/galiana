ELEGIE D'HIVER 

1. L'hiver est le reflux vers la source des voix.
Quelle lèvre altérée n'aura connu la source ?
Un vol de givre épars a revêtu les bois.
Les parfums de l'enfance ont renoué leur course.

2. Promesses. L'enfant mort n'abdiquera jamais,
Ni l'aurore têtue où s'agrippent ses rêves.
Un appel de chasseurs habite les sommets
Et l'âge est un défi, si les ans sont des trêves.

3. Je t'ai promis l'azur et l'éclat des joyaux,
Un cœur dur à briser la lame d'espérance.
Je devais te bâtir, outre vents, terre et eaux,
Un château si parfait qu'y demeurât l'enfance.

4. Je reviens. Au lac blanc se flétrissent les joncs.
Des traces de pas morts s'égarent dans la neige.
Le vent sur mes oublis comme sur des donjons
Souffle, et croulent les blocs qui m'ailent et m'allègent.

5. Il n'est ru plus têtu qu'une larme d'enfant.
Il n'est chant obstiné plus qu'une cantilène.
J'ai remonté le cours, j'ai regagné mes ans.
J'ai remonté la place où fleurissait ma peine.
 
6. Les roses de jadis se courbent en berceau.
Voici le mur croulant, le jardin, la tonnelle.
Hiver, tisse la vitre où germe le rinceau.
Mes chants et mes regrets sont même ritournelle.

7. Repaires. L'œil est dur et le rire assassin.
Mauvais sonne ce rire aux accords de buccin.
L'ange et le diamant habitent le rebelle.
Voici le temps du fauve et celui de l'errant.
Ils ont brisé son  mur, ils ont hanté ses vents,
Mis au fond de ses yeux l'abîme qui le hèle.

8. J'avais promis l'azur à qui tenait le feu.
Lancelot démonté d'avoir quêté le lieu
Et n'avoir rapporté ni coupe, ni mémoire,
Ignoré de la reine et raillé du bouffon
Evoque ses départs et la gloire que font
Des vols de papillons au fronton de l'histoire.

9. L'hiver est cet enfant qui n'a jamais souri
Et dont le lit était de cartes, de voyages.
Il n'est pas de chemin qui ramène au pays.
Au filet des regrets, seuls les ans se sont pris
Et cet autre pays qu'on appelle un visage.

10. Outre chair, outre temps, j'entends monter des voix.
Elles parlent de froid, de départ et de mines.
Amis, brisez ce mur qui m'éloigne de moi
Et puisqu'un gel unit dans un bloc mille lois,
Hiver, poing qui m'étreins, sois perle et m'illumine.

14-15 février 1983
 
Michel Galiana (c) 1992


WINTER ELEGY

1. In winter your mind flows to the source of voices.
Which thirsty lip would not know the spot where they rose?
Scattered frost that the wind on the branches poises
Brings back the fragrances that charmed the child I was.

2. I swore once: the dead child in me never should die,
Nor should the stubborn dawn to which clung on its dream.
I hear a hunter's call sounding high in the sky.
And age is a challenge that the years hardly screen.

3. Child, I had promised you blue sky and sparkling jewels,
That my heart would resist the sharp blade of despair,
That I would build for you, beyond wind, clay and pools,
So perfect an abode that childhood could dwell there.

4. I return there and see: The banks of the white pond
Are strewn with withered rush, footprints going astray
On the snow. The wind swept oblivion's towers around
And cleared the shattered stones that obstructed my way.

5. No brook is stubborn like the tears a child has shed.
No song is obstinate like a lullaby hushed.
I made the brook flow back; I redeemed my years spent
And found again the place where my pain had flourished.

6. Roses of bygone times bend together to bowers.
Here is the crumbling wall, the garden, the arbour...
Winter spins on the panes thin foliation covers
And my songs and my sighs merge to the same clamour.

7. Nothing but mere hideouts! What bad glance on display! 
His naughty laugh resounds like the horn on Doomsday!
In his adamant soul with the angel a strife!
For beast and tramp in him the time to rise has come:
They have broken the wall, with the wind they have blown,
Have lit in his eyes abysses that in him thrive.

8. I had promised blue skies to you who had the fire.
But Lancelot returned, dismounted, from the quest
From which he brought nothing, no Grail or Golden Fleece,
Ignored by the Queen and subjected to jest;
Had nothing to boast of but the fraud gloriousness
Of butterflies fluttering around a frontispiece.

9. Winter is that poor child whose smile never could dawn,
On whose bed were scattered maps for distant careers.
Of all the routes you take, none may carry you home.
All you caught in your nets of regrets are the years
And this strange face of yours that time shaped of its loam.

10. Beyond flesh, beyond time I hear voices that rise.
They tell of journey's end, of frost and of decay.
My friends, destroy that wall that my better self hides
And since frost unites things that went each its own way
Winter, fist clutching me, be pearl, give me your lights!

14-15 February 1983

 Q. I would like a brief commentary touching upon the motifs that appear in this poem, bearing in mind that it is a birthday gift Michel (born January 27, 1933) is giving himself on his 50th birthday.

The motifs are not scattered: they form a coherent constellation around the idea of returning to the “source” at mid life.

1. Winter as the season of return
The opening line (st. 1)— "L’hiver est le reflux vers la source des voix" — sets the tone.
Winter is not death;
it is reflux, a movement backward: toward origins, toward childhood, toward the “source of voices,” meaning memory, vocation, and identity.
At fifty, Michel frames winter as the season of truth, when illusions fall away and the essential resurfaces.

2. The “dead child” who never abdicates
This is the poem’s emotional core.
The "enfant mort" (st. 2) is the child he once was, the child he failed to protect, the child whose dreams “cling to the stubborn dawn,”
and the child whose tear (str. 5) is “more stubborn than any stream.”
At fifty, Michel confronts the inner child as both wound and guardian.
The child is “dead,” but also imperishable — a paradox that defines the poem’s elegiac tone.

3. The promise of the “azure”
Twice (st.3 and 8) he recalls a vow:

The “azure” is childhood’s ideal, the pure sky of beginnings, the promise of transcendence, the unreachable horizon of youth.
At fifty, Michel judges himself against this vow. The poem becomes a ledger of kept and broken promises.

4. The return to the childhood landscape
The poem’s middle stanzas (st.4 & 6) revisit the "white lake, the crumbling wall, the garden, the arbour, the roses of bygone days".
This is not nostalgia; it is pilgrimage.

5. The rebel, the beast, the wanderer
Suddenly the poem darkens (st. 7):

These figures represent the adult self: hardened, rebellious, solitary, haunted by an abyss “set in the depths of his eyes.”

The Lancelot stanza (st. 8) is crucial. Lancelot returns dismounted, empty handed,

This is Michel’s self portrait at fifty: the quest undertaken, the Grail not found, the glory reduced to

At fifty, Michel asks not for youth, nor for absolution, but for illumination.

Élégie d’hiver is Michel’s birthday reckoning, a self gift that takes the form of a return to childhood, a confrontation with the adult shadow, a confession of failed quests and a final request for inner transmutation.

The motifs — winter, the dead child, the azure, the rebel, Lancelot, the abyss, the pearl — all converge on a single truth: At fifty, Michel stands at the midpoint of life, looking backward for origin and forward for meaning.
