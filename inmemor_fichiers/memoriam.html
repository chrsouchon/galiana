In Memoriam

 

1 Je dépose au tombeau mon enfance embaumée.2

Le cimetière est clos et n'y poussent de fleurs.

Le cyprès grandira sur la bière fermée

Où de lys dissipés flotteront les pâleurs.

 

2 Sur le marbre attiédi la verte sempervive

A l'hiver jettera ses pétales flétris,

Mais saura que toujours se lèvent et se suivent

Les printemps radieux et les longs hivers gris.

In Memoriam

 

1 I lay into its grave my embalmed childhood2

The graveyard is closed and no flowers grow there.

A cypress shall arise on the sealed coffin's wood;

Which scattered petals of pale lilies now cover.

 

2 On the cool marble shall green, everlasting plants

In winter abandon their withering petals,

Causing to alternate in eternal balance,

The glorious springtimes with the long, dull winters.

I Le bateau

 

1 Le bassin du jardin3, moins vaste que ses rêves,

Où l'enfant voit glisser les blancs voiliers d'azur,

Ouvre à ses appétits sans limites des grèves

Où lesprit libéré flotte jusquau grand mur.

 

 

 

2 Il lance d'une main timide, car il n'ose

Mêler sa course aux élans fous des autres nefs

La barque de bois dur dardant l'étrave rose

Vers le large où pleure le jet en remous brefs.

 

3 Vers l'infini si grand qu'à peine il imagine

Que le vaisseau hardi puisse atteindre le bord.

Dans un livre il a vu des gravures: les cimes

De blancs glaciers, des cases de bois, un grand fjord

 

4. Aux eaux vertes et des pêcheurs aux lourdes vestes

De cuir. Il se souvient aussi qu'il a pu voir

Les débris de vaisseaux sombrés dont il ne reste

Que le squelette vague avec des étais noirs.

 

5. Il contemple souvent les cartes amarantes,

La France rose, la Chine jaune, le vert

Chili, de clairs pays avec des noms qui chantent,

Et tout autour, le bleu poème de la mer.

6. On lui parle souvent de marins, de naufrages,

Robinson sur son île et Jean Bart le forban

Saute, la hâche au poing, le sabre d'abordage

Au côté, sombre oiseau planant dans les haubans,

 

7. Et lorsqu'il joue avec sa soeur dans la cuisine,

Un banc, c'est le navire et l'île, le buffet.

Oh, que souffle le vent. Que la houle divine

L'emporte au gré de ses caprices! C'en est fait.

 

8. Plus tard, il roulera, dédaignant les tempêtes,

Sur le mer toute bleue où luisent les poissons.

Il verra les grands noirs, les Indiens dont la tête

S'orne de plumes hautes, et les dards à poison.

 

9. Un jour, quand il aura navigué bien des lieues,

On criera "terre". Les Chinois aux grands chapeeaux

Leur porteront les porcelaines, les feurs bleues,

Et sur les mâts soudain voleront les oiseaux

 

10. D'or et de feu. Déjà, il connaît son navire

Comme ce noir et vert qui flotte. Sur son bord

Un marin bleu de plomb, qu'il envie et admire,

Contemple son reflet qui danse, coiffé d'or.

 

11. Lorsque, marins barbus, ils passeront la ligne,

L'équipage au complet dansera sur le pont;

Capitaine, la nuit, il cherchera les signes

Pour guider sa corvette à travers les limons,

 

12. Et les cris des enfants, lorsqu'au port on arrive

Ce sont des cris pareils qui montent aussitôt,

Comme un parfum bordant d'un vert rideau la rive-

Mais une voix lui dit: "Où donc est ton bateau"

 

13. Il est loin. On le voit, immobile et stupide,

Au milieu du bassin sans bornes, oscillant

Lorsqu'une voile vire et lui jette un rapide

Salut, remous railleur et frémissement blanc.

 

14. Et l'enfant qui tient peu à son joujou volage

Et pour qui contempler est un plaisir sans fin

Sent pourtant un remords traverser son jeune âge

Et se briser son coeur en sanglots enfantins.

I The Boat

 

1 The rimmed pond of the park3, less vast than is his dream,

Where the boy sees blue-hulled, white-sailed boats glide along,

Opens to his boundless appetite strands that seem

To let his mind float free right up to the wall-front.

 

 

 

 

 

 

2 His timid hand launches -and he dreads to venture

Into the wild dashes of the other vessels-

His small craft of hard wood that's pointing its pink stem

Towards the open sea where the jet spurts its swirls,

 

3 Towards a boundless space and he can't imagine

That the gallant vessel could ever come to berth.

In a book he saw once pictures of breathtaking

Peaks, of white glaciers, wooden huts, of a firth

 

4. With green waters and men in thick leather jackets.

He also remembers that he has seen, one day,

Remains of wrecked ships of which nothing was left

But some vague skeletons propped up against black stays.

 

5. He often considers some amaranthine map

Showing France in pink hue, China in white, Chile

In green, and bright countries that would his dreams entrap,

And all around, the blue poetry of the vast sea.

6. He was often told of sailormen and shipwrecks,

Robinson on his isle and Jean Bart the corsair

Who bounces, with the ax in his fist, his cutlass

By his side, from the shrouds, like some bird in the air.

 

7. And when with his sister he plays in the kitchen,

A bench becomes a ship and the chest an island.

The wind may blow in gusts. Now he shall be taken

By the capricious swell which he will not withstand.

 

8. Later on he shall roll, disregarding the gales,

On the deep, blue ocean, full of glittering fish.

He will see Africans and red Indians who wear

Feather hair-dresses and poison darts in a sheath.

 

9. Some day, when he will have travelled for miles and miles,

The watch will cry "Land ho!". The Chinese with wide caps

Will bring on board dishes of porcelain, blue flowers,

And birds of gold and fire will flutter round the masts.

 

10. And he knows already his proud ship's appearance:

Like that black and green one floating there with, aboard,

That blue lead sailor whom he admires and his glance,

Rests on his reflection amidst sparkles of gold.

 

11. When, long-bearded sailors, they'll be crossing the line,

All the men of the crew will dance upon the deck;

As their captain he will search the sky for the signs

That will lead all along through the silts the corvette,

 

12. And the cries of children when they'll reach the harbour

Will be sensed as soon as they come within earshot,

Like a scent that will seam with a green hem the shore...

But suddenly a voice asks him "Where is your boat?"

 

13. Far she is. Motionless and stupid, look at her,

In the very middle of the boundless basin.

When a sail nearby tacks and beckons her over

The white, shuddering wash gives her a mocking swing.

 

14. Though the boy little cares about his fickle toy,

He feels a remorseful urge rise in his young age,

And though contemplating is to him endless joy

He feels his heart burst into tears of childish rage.

II Soir dAutomne

 

1. Quand l'automne venait, émondant par les rues

Et les jardins les marroniers et les tilleuls,

Avec l'odeur lourde et grise, toujours accrue,

Flottait comme un désir étrange d'être seul

 

2. Près des flaques luisant où semble-t-il se mire

Le vent (quel souvenir se plisse en son miroir?)

Et de sentir comme un charme qu'on ne peut dire

Le jour d'ombre noirci se fondre dans le soir.

 

3. Et puis,la lampe est presque intime. Sur la table

C'est un rond chaud que guette en silence la nuit.

Sorciers, goules, gros loup et bonhomme de sable

S'amassent dans les coins, nous observent sans bruit.

 

4. Le sommeil qui tombait allumait les mystères.

Au son du piano montait les rêves fous

Et c'était cavalcade horrible et éphémère,

Un ogre chevauchant l'odeur grasse des choux.

II Autumn Evening

 

1. When Autumn came and trimmed everywhere in the street

And the gardens the linden and the chestnut tree

With a heavy and dull fragrance allways increased,

There floated about a strange urge lone to be

 

2. Near gleaming puddles where could the wind, one would tell,

Mirror itself (which memory wrinkles that glass?)

And to feel, like a strange and ineffable spell,

The shadowing daylight merging into the night.

 

3. And then, there was the lamp that made you feel cosy.

On the table the warm spot circled by the night.

Sorcerers, witches, wolf and the nasty bogey

Gathered in the corners and kept a watchful eye.

 

4. Slumber came then and would arouse some mystery.

The sound of the piano caused foolish dreams to rise

And it was, in a dreadful, short-lasting hurry,

An ogre riding on the fat smell of the kales.

III Le laboureur

 

1. C'est le chant des labours, c'est le chant de l'automne.

La classe est grise où passe un laboureur tordu4

Poussant le soc. Chant des labours. Chant monotone

Et poignant, que la classe entonne. Où entendu?

 

2. Je sais qu'il est là-bas où s'envole mon âme,

Un champ. Tombe le soir. Poussant ses animaux,

Spectre sombre, fleur des labours et des semailles,

Un laboureur erre sous un vol de corbeaux.

 

3. Le chant se fait plus grave. O bucoliques tendres.

Près du buisson raidi d'où monte le brouillard

La bergère de vent et le berger de cendre

Songent aux grands boeufs roux qui reviennent bien tard.

 

4. Il neige et dans la cour volent des formes blanches:

O soir, feu clair et doux, grasse odeur de sarments.

Les souvenirs tombent en molles avalanches

Des cieux où souriaient des yeux étrangement

 

5. Limpides. Dans le champ dresse vers la lumière

Ses deux bras la charrue. Il chante, il neige et l'air

Semble pétri de souvenirs et d'aube claire.

C'est le chant des labours - c'est le chant des hivers.

III The Ploughman

 

1. Hark! It's the ploughing song, it's the song of the fall.

In the dull class-room, on the notebooks a ploughman,4

Bent down, pushes the share. Ploughing song, dreary call

That the class strike up, where did I to you listen?

 

2. I know that down yonder wither my soul takes flight,

There's a field on which night is falling. Sombre ghost,

Surging, pushing his beasts, at ploughing and seed time

A ploughman is roaming under a flight of crows .

 

3. The song becomes deeper, like some pastoral tunes.

Near the stiffened thicket over which rise the mists

The sheep girl clad in wind, the shepherd clad in fumes

Wait for the red oxen and blow on their numb fists.

 

4. It's snowing and white shapes heap up on the play ground:

O evening, bright and soft fire, fat smell of twig.

Memories, wooly flakes slowly swirling around

In the skies where eyes smile that are strangely limpid!

 

5. On the field stands the plough and raises to the light

Its two arms. A lilting voice is heard. It's snowing.

The air of memories is full and with dawn bright.

It was a ploughing song - now shall wintertime sing.

IV Lorelei

 

1. Vous qui chantiez si bien, nixe au sourire étrange,5

Le beau sapin d'argent rêvant l'ombre du Rhin

Tandis que monte un chant obscur des fonds de fange

Où dort l'oiseau sacré - vous qui chantiez si bien.

 

2. Vous qui chantiez si bien qu'on aurait dit que l'âme

Des grands roseaux secrets où frémit le matin

Dans vos regards avait glissé comme une flamme

Leur humide clarté - vous qui chantiez si bien.

 

3. Vous qui chantiez si bien que dans l'albe lumière,

Lentement dénouant vos cheveux sans que rien

Sur votre chant vainqueur jette son noir mystère,

Je vous voyais trembler - vous qui chantiez si bien.

 

4. Vous qui chantiez si bien quand se mouvaient les ombres

Fluides qui pleuvaient sur votre corps serein,

Vous qui laissiez si bien, Musique où rien n'est sombre,

Votre chant se mêler au rythme aérien -

Vous qui chantiez si bien - moi qui rêvait si bien.

IV Lorelei

 

1. You, whose song was so fine, siren with a strange smile5

Under the silver fir whose dream shaded the Rhine

While a deadened chant rose among the grounds of mire

Where the sacred bird sleeps - you, whose song was so fine.

 

2. You, whose song was so fine that it seemed that the soul

Of the tall secret reeds where the morning winds whine

Into your eyes had sunk, like a flame and left whole

Their watery brightness - you, whose song was so fine.

 

3. You, whose song was so fine that in the white halo,

When slowly you undid your hair and though no pain

On your triumphant chant would throw a dark shadow,

I saw that you quivered - you, whose song was so fine.

 

4. You, whose song was so fine when the shade was moving

And down your placid shape ran like some mellow wine,

You, whose melodious song that no gloom was filling,

With ethereal warble could so deftly combine -

You, whose song was so fine, my dream that was so fine.

V Les cygnes

 

1. Dans le bassin voyez glisser deux cygnes blanches

Leur col est comme un luth6, leur aile comme un chant

Qui s'ouvre. Au fond du flot, les cygnes qui se penchent

Voient que glisse l'ondin de rêve, doux - méchant-

 

2. Méchant car il a su que les cygnes tremblantes

Lorsqu'un bel étranger admire leur candeur

Frémissent, et dressant au ciel leurs âmes, chantent,

Comme si sur le chant s'envolait leur splendeur.

 

3. L'ondin, beau comme un lys, par les roseaux complices,

S'en vint, et dans sa main brillait le fil d'argent

Qui devait ramener, belles de leur supplice,

Les cygnes qui miraient leur blancheur en nageant.

 

4. Il les a ramenées, sauvages prisonnières,

Le couteau dans sa main chanta: la tête chut.

Tandis qu'au ciel montait une blanche prière,

Le col aux doigts sanglants se tordit comme un luth.

V The Swans

 

1. Look on that pond those two white swans gliding along!

Their necks are like a lute6, their wings a serenade

That rises. The swans see in the depths of the pond

A dream watersprite swim who is mild -who is bad-

 

2. Who is bad, for he knows that the shuddering swans,

When a handsome stranger beholds their white feather

Quiver, and raising to the skies their souls, will chant,

As if, with their song, would soar up all their splendour.

 

3. The bright watersprite came through the treacherous reeds

Concealing in his hand the gleaming silver bond

That was designed to catch, after fair agonies,

The swans that liked to swim and mirror on the pond.

 

4. And he caught them and they were his wild prisoners.

The knife sings: the heads fall under the slabs that shoot.

Whilst to heaven arose immaculate prayers,

The necks, in gory hands, wrang and became a lute.

VI Les lauriers

 

1. Lorsqu'au fond des grands bois tout roses de lumière

J'entendis la chanson que vient clore la mort

Je le savais que ce n'était pas la première

Mais pensais que nul chant ne serait aussi fort.

 

2. Lampe jaune, réduit intime - folle enfance.

Vous n'irez plus au bois, les lauriers sont coupés,7

Les beaux lauriers qui couronneraient sa jouvence,

Et la dame ne viendra pas les ramasser.

 

3. Elle ne croisera pas les mains sur les branches,

Les ronces pousseront et les grands glailleuls fous.

Les lauriers chus, hélas, enguirlandent ses hanches,

Les liserons d'un soir enlacent ses genoux.

 

4. Et seul me reste un souvenir comme de fièvres:

Sa voix rauque d'enfant, de fiertés embrasée,

Son rire s'embaumant d'effluves sur ses lèvres,

Sa bouche où des garçons s'égarent les baisers.

 

5. Les lauriers sont coupés. Il n'y a plus de rondes.

Il n'y a plus d'enfant pour rêver tout un soir.

Adieu, janviers givrés! Adieu, l'automne blonde!

Tous deux nous poursuivons notre course au bois noir.

VI The Laurels

 

1. When under yonder wood bathing in rosy tone

I first perceived the song that's concluded by death,

Well did I know that it could not be the first one

But thought that no chant could ever rise from such depth.

 

2. Yellow lamp, intimate hideout - foolish childhood.

You won't go to the wood, the laurel shrubs are cut,7

The lovely laurels with which would be wreathed his youth,

And the lady never will come to pick them up.

 

3. She will never again fold her hands on the leaves,

Bramble branches will thrive and wild gladioli.

Fallen branches, alas, entwine around her hips,

Bindweed has ground and bound her two knees overnight.

 

4. And naught is left to me but feverish memories:

Her hoarse little girl's voice where pride kindles a fire,

Her laugh that on her lips fragrance accompanies,

Her mouth on which the kisses of the boys run dry.

 

5. The laurel shrubs are cut. There are no more dances.

And no more child at night for dreaming in the mood,

Farewell, frosty winters! Farewell, golden autumns!

Now each of us goes his own way through the dark wood.

VII Six dames

 

1. Six dames sont venues au jardin de l'aurore;

Toutes portaient le voile et tenaient dans la main

La fleur qui se fanant doit reverdir encore.

Mais est-il un jadis lorsqu'il n'est de demain?

 

2. La première, le front ceint de myrtes antiques

Luttait de ses pieds nus avec le marbre noir.

Du théorbe sacré s'exhalait le cantique

Etrange quand la croix rayonna sur Assur.

 

3. La seconde menait les moutons blancs en laisse

Et son chant s'égrenait en perles par les prés.

Sa joue était le vent et son front la jeunesse.

De muguet frais éclos son cou était paré.

 

4. La troisième tendit la rouge violette

Où le sang a perlé de mes ans flagellés.

Le dédain se figeait sur sa bouche muette

Et le regret poignant des espoirs envolés.

 

5. La quatrième, ou bien n'était-ce que le songe

D'un soupir, a passé dans le soleil de mai.

Elle portait le thym comme une autre colombe

Et dans le ciel bientôt son vol s'est refermé.

 

6. La cinquième a semé les lilas sur ma route.

Soirs de juin, rêves fous, sourires entrevus

Et toujours rappelés, soir vibrant qu'on écoute,

Instants, ivresses, que n'êtes-vous revenus?

 

7. La sixième un matin s'est levée sur mon âme.

La nuit avec les chants l'engloutit pour toujours

Et mon esprit, guettant l'inextinguible flamme,

Attend, las et muet, au sommet de sa tour.

 

8. Six dames qui pourtant n'ont qu'un même visage

Dans le même jardin se sont venues asseoir.

Des fleurs, emblêmes vains, luisent à leur corsage,

Mais leurs traits effacés se perdent dans le soir.

9. Dans le même jardin six dames en mystère

Sont venues, se sont tues et s'éloignent sans bruit.

La Septième viendra. Ce sera la première,8

La Seule, seule comme l'Etoile ou la Nuit.

VII Six Ladies

 

1. Six ladies have come to the garden of the dawn

Each of them wore a veil; each of them brought with her

A bloom which, once withered, was to grow green again.

But are there times of old when there is no future?

 

2. The first one whose brow was wreathed with antique myrtle,

Barefooted was treading upon the stainless stone.

She played on the sacred theorbo the subtle

Hymn that was heard when o'er Assur the Cross had shone.

 

3. And the second was leading white sheep on a leash

Notes of her song fell one by one on the meadow.

And her cheek was like wind and like youth was her flesh.

Lily of the valley adorned her graceful brow.

 

4. The third one was holding out a crimson violet

On which the blood of my scourged years have spilt drops.

She came forth; her mouth was disdainfully silent.

On her weighed heartrending regrets and vanished hopes.

 

5. The fourth one -but perhaps it was the dream only

Of a sigh- has  passed by in the sun light of May.

She hold a twig of thyme and seemed a dove to be

And in the misty sky she had soon flown away.

 

6. The fifth one strewed lilac blossoms along my path.

Eves of June, foolish dreams, smiles that were smiled in vain

And always remembered, vibrating evenings that

One hears, hours of rapture, you'll never come again!

 

7. The sixth one on my soul has risen one morning.

The night with its rumors devoured it for ever

And my mind that watches the flare's steady burning,

Waits weary and silent, on the top of the tower.

 

8. Six ladies -yet they have the same and unique face-

To the same garden came and they were sitting here.

Flowers, futile emblems, adorned in vain their grace:

Her blurred features vanish as the evening draws near.

9. To the same garden six ladies stealthily came.

They came, they kept silent, were soon out of my sight.

The Seventh one shall come, as the first one the same,8

She is the unique one, as is the Star, the Night.

VIII Aux étoiles

 

1. Beaux regards constellant  le ciel de ma jeunesse,

Vous avez rayonné au printemps de mes jours.

Regards mystérieux et doux, frissons, caresses,

Secrets moins murmurés que rêvés, bas et sourds

 

2. Beaux regards, pleins d'émois et de luttes muettes,

Lourds de baumes qui ruisseliez sur mon berceau,

Radieux, d'où tombaient la paix et les tempêtes,

Perdus le soir, et chaque nuit encor plus beaux.

 

3. Etoiles qui le soir vous levez sur mes songes,

J'ai suivi vos appels et les suivrai toujours.

Brillez, regards, sans fin, sur les flots où je plonge.

Etoiles du marin, vous garderez mes jours.

VIII To the Stars

 

1. Fair glances with which were studded my youth's heavens,

You beamed forth, enlightning the springtime of my life.

Mysterious glances, soft, caressing shudders,

Secrets that are not said but dreamed in a low voice.

 

2. Fair glances, full of stir and of silent contest,

Heavy with balms flooding my cradle all over,

Irradiating glances, whence fell peace or tempest,

Lost every eve, you would grow every night fairer.

 

3. Stars, that in the evening would rise over my dreams,

I followed your calls and will always follow them.

Sparkle, glances, sparkle endlessly, on those streams

Where I bathe, sailor's stars, of my days be guardian.

IX La maison du matin

 

1 La maison du matin9 qu'en treilles de lumière

La vigne enguirlandait et les fruits lourds et chauds,

Dont les parfums berçaient les guêpes familières

Comme aux flots léthéens l'âme ivre de repos.

 

2 La maison du matin au sourd manteau sonore,

Le loquet immobile et les volets baissés,

En son coeur d'ombre et de calme laissait éclore

Le silence aux parfums plus doux que des baisers.

 

3 Mais un jour est venu frapper la visiteuse,

Jusqu'alors inconnue et messagère dont

Les doigts versèrent sur le toit la foi pieuse

Et firent se lever le sévère pardon.

 

4 Aux murs on vit grandir et croître comme un lierre

La treille de velours lamé d'argent au bord.

La maison du matin attend dans la lumière,

La maison du repos, la maison de la mort

IX House of the Morning

 

1 House of the morning9 that, like a sunny arbour

A vine clad in garlands. Heavy and lukewarm fruit,

With scents that lulled the tame wasps humming with ardour

As did the Lethe's waves the rest-drunken spirit.

 

2 House of the morning wrapped in a dull coat of sound,

The latch was standing still and the shutters were locked,

In its heart full of shades and of quietness was found

Silence with a fragrance, like some kisses, so soft.

 

3 Until, once, a woman came and knocked at the door.

She was of all unknown and was a messenger

Whose fingers shed over the roof pious fervour

And caused stern fotgiveness to be requested there.

 

4 On the walls started to grow, some ivy alike,

The vine with its silver edged leaves of velvet.

The house of the morning is waiting in the light,

The house of Rest which is also a house of Death.

X The Bells

 

1 The bells10 have been sounding the candid birth anthem.

Life on your finger has slipped its ponderous ring,

But a still deeper song vibrates in your bosom

And gleams like some water in your childish babbling.

 

2 The bells are now sounding the bright wedding anthem.

The groom on your finger will slip the only ring.

All that was - and was loved - all is burnt in a flame

And the flower-dotted path to the grave is leading.

 

3 The bells some day will sound the dull burial anthem.

The groom on gnarled fingers will slip the final ring

And the deep and panting death rattle will quieten

Down and will in the grave's final peace at last sink.

 

4 The bells were giving rythm on your way to your steps.

Each ring of them has thrown away one of your hours

To the hours spent, and what you heard were all regrets

Of those unpicked and for ever withered flowers.

XIV Les noces de Figaro

 

1. Mozart qui le savait suspendit la romance.

L'âme qui s'éveillait sentit mourir un peu

Son rêve, et s'élever au milieu du silence

Son chant intérieur qui dort, silencieux.

 

2. Chérubin, coeur volant, n'a pas plus de jeunesse.

Suzanne ne soupire pas avec un chant

Plus clair - si clair qu'il fond dans l'azur et ne laisse

Que le regret d'un moment furtif et touchant.

 

3. Sous les grands marronniers va passer une étoile.

O Comte, oublie à tout jamais le valet bas,

La nuit frissonne sous le regard de Rosine

Et les fleurs deviennent des perles sous ses pas.

 

4. Bartholo disparaît. Il n'est plus de Basile.

Marceline se peigne au delà du décor.

Tout est musique. Au coeur brûlant, elle scintille.

L'orchestre au loin la suit comme l'oeil de l'amour.

S'éveillera du long mirage de carton

XIV Figaros Wedding

 

1. Mozart, who was aware, interrupted the chant.

The awakening soul felt its dream fade away,

And amidst the stillness it heard rise the silent

Innermost, sleeping song that crooned, gentle and gay.

 

2. Fickle Cherubino has not more youthfulness.

Susanna does not sigh nor sing a clearer chant

- So clear that it merges in the ether and leaves

Behind but the regret of a stealthy moment.

 

3. Beneath the tall chestnuts a star presently falls.

Count, let for ever be forgotten the flunkey,

The night shudders shily, which Rosina beholds

And flowers are turning to pearls along her way.

 

4. Bartholo disappears. There's no more Basilio.

Marcellina prepares at the back of the stage.

All is music, sparkling, enthralling with brio.

And words of diamonds follow it in cortege.

 

5. The divine aria trembles upon the lips,

Followed in the distance by loving instruments

Which go their drowsy way and are caught on the green

Thorns that thrive on the path and are joyful moments;

 

6. Moments for which will last the brief smile one dreams of

For long, that in the night dances and that, at dawn,

One finds beside his bed, that one's memory haunts;

That never will be more than happiness bygone.

 

7. Then hopes will be muted and then will the Countess

Awake again from her lengthy dream of cardboard;

And in the skies the song will sound full of sadness -

Sad like roses withered while they were still in bud.

XVII Mirabeau Bridge

 

1. Under Mirabeau Bridge13 the Seine in the shade flows

The majestic lighthouse makes a hole in the night.

I don't want to be washed along your tide, O crowd,

Nor don't I want to be, lighthouse, lost in the dark.

 

2. My life is mine and my verse tells of its rapture.

I force on it rythm and my fingers beat time

And around the cry of a caress I conjure

My pitiless craft shall strong chains of bronze entwine.

 

3. Come. The flow is nothing and the night but a dream,

And the roaring city, and the earth and the sky

Are not more than the mist my spirit wraps up in

And that an odious gust of wind could nullify.

 

4. I feel a secret joy in fruitless proceedings,

Despise the mean-minded and I am proud to have

Dared what the vile rabble never could even dream,

And my dark pride at you shall let me rant and rave.

A spit in our bodies will rotate and desire

And to rest on, we like beds of nettles and thorns.

Our innermost beings are delighted when they are

7. Under Mirabeau Bridge the Seine in the shade flows.

Thousand lights are spread over the town's dark attire.

A bronze was cast of which both of us were the moulds.

XX Sunday

 

1. The bell has rung. The church door opens to the light.

And in rushes a swarm of gay, blue butterflies

That shudder and flutter their wings and leave behind

Festive perfumes. And now, in everybody's eyes

 

2. Endless Sunday sparkles. On their arms the housewives

Heavily burdened, wear the bread and the Sunday

Cakes and scan their steps on the bell's concluding chimes,

Fresh minuets like those in the manors they play.

 

3. Then the bell stands silent. All vanishes. The pub

Fills up with flower hats, Sunday clothes, Sunday peace

And the Church square is soon void where the tender sun

Looks for a last face on which to blow a last kiss.

XXI Neige

 

1. La neige n'a voilé que les chaumes flétris -

Mais le silence dort, bercé de songes blancs.

Vous n'irez plus - tu n'iras plus - par les prairies

Et pourtant, avec la blancheur qui luit en moi

Les souvenirs s'en vont, comme après une fête

Un regret, une larme au fond, presqu'un émoi.

 

3. La neige n'a voilé que les rosiers sans roses -

Les parfums triomphants s'enfoncent dans la nuit.

Parfums, ramenez-nous, avec des fleurs écloses,

Un peu de ce qui fut et fuit.

XXI Snow

 

1. The snow only did hide away the stubble fields-

But bred slumbering silence, cradled by candid dreams.

You shall - thou shalt - never again walk through the meads

Nor shall ever the birds answer the chants you sing.

 

2. The snow only did hide away the flat countries -

And yet the whiteness that has invaded my mind

Has dismissed, like after a feast, my memories

And left behind regrets and tears, hardly refrained.

 

3. The snow only did hide rose trees without roses -

And once heady perfumes sink now into the night.

Perfumes, bring back to us with the fresh-blown flowers,

A little of the past gone by.
