XI NERVAL

1. La blanche Aurélia chantait dans une étoile.
Les rondes de jadis tournaient au creux des bois.
L'antique fiancée rougissait dans ses voiles
Et l'ultime corbeau croassait sur le toit

2. Un refrain a suffi pour héler la détresse
Et faire resplendir, ô coeur harmonieux,
Profil athénien, le spectre de jeunesse
Qui rend triste et brise les plaisirs et les jeux.

3. Un matin, dans les bois, tu cherchais les vestiges,
De tes ans. Les clochers vibraient comme toujours.
L'abîme du destin te soufflait le vertige -
Mais la sainte pleurait encore ses dieux sourds.

4. Et les cloches en vain célébraient l'âme saine.
Un vol blanc de rayons ravit Aurélia.
Sylvie aux bois flétris vit mourir la fontaine
Et l'anneau des accords à l'autre la lia.

5. L'éternelle épousée attendait en silence,
Errante pèlerine aux lisières du temps.
Elle savait le mot, elle dit la sentence.
Une étoile jaillit vers les cieux éclatants.

6. Et l'errant, le songeur, le voyant de l'espace,
Sachant que dans son coeur Dieu s'était endormi
Remonta comme un chant vers les cieux blancs de grâce.
Sur la terre sonnaient les cloches de Loisy.

Michel Galiana (c) 1953-1954


XI NERVAL

1. Singing within a star was Aurélia the Pale
The round dances of old were turning in the grove
The ancient fiancée blushed beneath her veils,
And the belated crow croaked upon the roof.

2. A refrain was enough to summon up distress
And to make resplendent — O harmonious heart —
Athena’s profile, the spectre of youthfulness
That sadly keeps pleasures and games, and you, apart.

3. One morning, in the woods, you searched for traces
Of your years. The steeples chimed as they always do.
Vertigo breathed on your unfathomable fate
And the Saint began mourning her deaf gods anew.

4. And the bells in vain celebrated the sane soul.
Aurélia disappeared: A flight of rays took her.
Sylvie, in withered woods, saw the fountain turn foul,
And the ring of accords bound her to the other.

5. And the eternal bride awaited in silence,
A wandering pilgrim at the edges of time.
And since she knew the word, she uttered the sentence.
And a star sprang from dusk toward the blazing shine.

6. And the wanderer, the dreamer, the seer of space,
Knowing that in his heart God henceforth was drowsy,
Rose again like a chant towards white skies of grace,
While on earth resounded the far bells of Loisy.

I. Aurélia — the Visionary Woman, the Star of Madness
Michel opens with:
La blanche Aurélia chantait dans une étoile.
This is a direct transposition of Nerval’s Aurélia, the visionary beloved who appears in dreams, visions, hallucinations, and celestial epiphanies. In Nerval, Aurélia is the woman of revelation, the star woman, the epiphanic beloved, the figure of mystical madness.
Michel preserves all of this: Aurélia sings, she is pale (Nerval’s “blanche vision”), she is placed inside a star, she is the first apparition of the poem.
Michel’s Aurélia is not a character; she is a cosmic event. Her disappearance in stanza 4 — ravit Aurélia — mirrors Nerval’s own loss of the visionary beloved and the collapse of his inner world. Michel uses Aurélia as the axis of revelation: the poem begins with her light and ends with her disappearance.

II. Sylvie — the Woman of Memory and the Dying Fountain
In stanza 4:
Sylvie aux bois flétris vit mourir la fontaine.
This is a perfect Nervalian gesture. In Sylvie, the fountain is the symbol of youth, memory, rural innocence, the lost world of Valois. When Michel writes la fontaine mourut, he is reenacting Nerval’s own heartbreak: the moment when the idealized past collapses, when the “real” Sylvie cannot match the dream Sylvie, when memory itself becomes mortal.
Michel’s Sylvie is placed in withered woods, witnessing the death of the fountain, bound by the ring of accords to “the other” (Nerval’s painful recognition that Sylvie belongs to another man).
This is Michel’s most faithful Nervalian moment: he stages the death of memory.
Aurélia = the death of vision. Sylvie = the death of memory.

Michel never names Artémis explicitly — but she is unmistakably present in stanza 5:
L’éternelle épousée attendait en silence, Errante pèlerine aux lisières du temps.
This is Nerval’s Artémis, the goddess of the forbidden hour (“La treizième revient…”), the eternal bride who appears at the threshold of time, the woman who is both virgin and fatal, both epiphany and doom.
Michel’s “éternelle épousée” is the goddess bride, the wanderer at the edges of time, the one who “knows the word” and “pronounces the sentence.”
This is pure Nerval: Artémis is the one who reveals destiny, who speaks the fatal word, who governs the visionary threshold.
Michel’s star that “springs toward the blazing heavens” is the Artémis star, the moment of revelation that ends Nerval’s poem Artémis.
Thus Michel’s three women form a Nervalian triad: Aurélia — vision, Sylvie — memory, Artémis — revelation.
This is Nerval’s entire feminine metaphysics, reconstructed.

Conclusion
