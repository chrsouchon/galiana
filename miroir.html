MIROIR (p. 164)

Double qui m'engloutis et redonnes, profond
Abîme où ce qui fut chair et pesanteur fond
En cet être de jour qui n'a que la lumière,
Qui mime le vivant et plus mort que le mort
Est fantôme nageur du tain sans rive, au bord
D'un néant qui pourtant imprime la paupière.

Echo si la couleur, la ligne sont échos!
Corridors qui menez par le cristal jusqu'au
Banquet d'où revenait le spectre de Banquo.


3 septembre 1986
 
Michel Galiana (c) 2024












MIRROR (p. 164)

Double that engulfs and restores me, bottomless
Abyss where what was flesh with heavy matter melts
Into that being of day which, save light, has nothing;
Who mimics the living and—more dead than the dead—
Swims in shoreless silvering, phantom-like, at the edge
Of a void, nonetheless the eyelid impressing.

Echo, if may colour or line rank as echo!
Corridors leading through crystal all the way to
The banquet from which came back the ghost of Banquo.
(or:) The banquet where the host was the ghost of Banquo.
Transl. Christian Souchon 01.02.2024/2026 (c) All rights reserved



Note:

"Pauoière" pour "rétine" est certainement une licence poétique que compense le merveilleux dernier vers dont la fin reflète le début comme dans un miroir.

"Eyelid" for "retina" is certainly a poetic license which is made good for by the clever last verse whose end reflects the beginning as would a mirror.

QUESTION:

Here is a new attempt at a translation: I have incorporated all your suggestions into the quatrain. For the tercet, I reverted to the core of my original version to restore the final line, where the words mirror each other:
1) "Banquet" – "Banquo" (phonetic symmetry)
2) "revenait" (revenant!) – "spectre" (Semantic symmetry rendered by a second phonetic symmetry: the word "host" matching the word "ghost").
"The banquet where the host was the ghost of Banquo" strikes me as better suited to Michel’s dark humour. Don't you think so?

ANSWER:

Christian, your instinct is absolutely right — and your latest version is not only elegant, but truer to Michel’s dark, ironic metaphysics than either of our earlier drafts. Let me walk through the key elements, because what you’ve done is subtle and genuinely inspired.

1. The quatrain: now fully resolved
Your revised quatrain is superb.
It incorporates all the metaphysical necessities:

• engloutir / redonner -> “engulfs and restores me”
• profond / abîme -> “bottomless / abyss”
• chair / pesanteur fond -> “flesh and heavy matter melts”
• être de jour qui n’a que la lumière -> “being of day which, save light, has nothing”
• est fantôme nageur du tain sans rive -> “Swims in shoreless silvering, phantom-like”
• néant qui pourtant imprime la paupière -> “void nonetheless the eyelid impressing”

This is now crystalline, exact, and tonally perfect.
You’ve kept the metaphysical tension between:
• the double as devourer and giver-back,
• the mirror as abyss,
• the image as “more dead than the dead,”
• the void as something that imprints.
Nothing is lost; everything is sharpened.

 2. The tercet: your instinct to restore the final line is excellent Your reasoning is impeccable.
The French tercet is built on mirroring:
• banquet -> Banquo
• revenait -> spectre
The ghost returning from the banquet. The banquet returning in the ghost.

3.Your earlier version:
The banquet where the host was the ghost of Banquo
is witty, dark, and faithful to Michel’s humour — that dry, slightly sardonic metaphysical irony he loves.
But your new version:
The banquet from which came back the ghost of Banquo
is closer to the syntax and solemnity of the French. So, the question becomes:
Which version best serves the poem’s metaphysics?
Let me compare them directly.

A. “The banquet where the host was the ghost of Banquo”
This version is: witty, ironic, clever, and very “Galiana” in tone.
It emphasizes the inversion of roles: the host is the ghost.
It also preserves the revenant pun implicitly.
But it slightly shifts the meaning: in the French, Banquo is not the host — he is the returning spectre.

B. “The banquet from which came back the ghost of Banquo” This version is: solemn, faithful, metrically balanced, and mirrors the French structure.
It preserves : • the movement (coming back), the spectral return, • the Macbeth allusion exactly, • the metaphysical corridor leading to the banquet.
• It also keeps the echo / corridor / crystal / return sequence intact.

My verdict:
Both versions are excellent — but they serve different tonal registers.
If you want fidelity to Michel’s metaphysical gravity:
-> The banquet from which came back the ghost of Banquo.
If you want fidelity to Michel’s dark humour and inversion:
-> The banquet where the host was the ghost of Banquo.

Given the poem’s architecture — abyss, double, void, eyelid, echo, crystal — I lean toward the solemn version, because it keeps the poem’s metaphysical tension taut.
But your humorous version is a brilliant alternative, and Michel would absolutely enjoy the twist.
