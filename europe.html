XXII L'EUROPE ET SES CONVULSIONS

Or le vaisseau roulait, donnant de la bande,
Car les boulets l'avaient presque dématé,
Les cales pleines d'eau et du cri des bandes
Dont les rixes ne savaient de répit, et

Toutefois toujours Eldorado des hères
Du lumignon phébéen démodé fous,
Asile quiet quand s'ouvraient les cratères
Et grossissait le flot des ventres, des foules

Immaîtrisées, et les regards des bêtes
Trouaient la paix de nos tumultes huilés-
Muezzin dansant sur les brocarts d'une fête,
Rêve interdit du drapeau bleu étoilé,

Glus empêtrant les colombes et les aubes,
Jungles célant les fuites et les charniers,
Beaux mots ailés qui êtes leurres et daubes,
Fus-je fou de vous avoir toujours niés?

La fille passait à la crête de soufre.
Giton paradait, les bras marbrés, le sang
Pourri. Le vieux chant rejoignait dans le gouffre
Péans défunts, alexandrin impuissant.

Puis le jour vint où mourut la chansonnette.
Jacques s'assit sur les marches du palais
Tendant son claque ou moyennant sa sornette.
Les vieux démons qu'on avait cru en allés

Raclèrent les guitares aux promenades
Pour empaumer le passant moins démuni
Ou, sans accords, chroniquèrent leur panade.
Ce fut le temps de Chloë sans jupon ni

Pudique atour s'étalant sur les murailles.
Daphnis touchait les jetons de son repos.
Les grognons parlèrent de funérailles 
Du vieux Gallus qui n'avait plus que sa peau

A lui. Le conte où Sade rejoint Dracula
Fit le bonheur de l'affranchi Bonhomet
Passé Néron, et l'ancien monde bascula
Au chaos que d'aucuns dirent sommet.

Michel Galiana (c) 1990


XXII EUROPEAN CONVULSIONS

Back then the vessel was rolling, listing badly,
For the cannonballs had almost dismantled her.
Her holds were full of water and of cries uttered
By gangs whose brawls would burst out repeatedly

Yet it was still an Eldorado for wretches,
Madmen of the outdated Phoebean lantern,
A quiet asylum still when craters opened
That swelled the tide of bellies, of unmastered

crowds of populace and the eyes of the beasts
Pierced the peace of our lubricated tumults —
A muezzin danced on the brocades of a feast,
And the forbidden dream of the starred blue flag,

Birdlimes meant to catch peace doves and morning glare,
Jungles with mass graves, where fugitives hid away
Ye, fair words, winged words that are but lures and snares —
Was it folly to have denied you always?

And the girl passed along with her crest of sulphur.
The Nancy-boys strutted, marble-armed, rotten-
blooded. The old art of song joined in the gulf
The dead paeans, the impotent alexandrine.

Until the day arrived when the ditty died.
A jackass sat down on the steps of the king’s hall,
Holding out his top hat, peddling his beggar’s lies.
The old demons and fiends we believed had left all

Scraped their guitars now along the passageways
To charm the passer‑by and rob his last bank-note
Or, without any chords, chronicled their decay
It was then that Chloë without her petticoat,

Without modest attire, sprawled on the walls.
While Daphnis fingered his money, just retired.
Grumblers set to mutter about the funerals
Of old Gallus, who had nothing left but his hide

for himself. The tale in which Sade meets Dracula
Delighted the emancipated rabble’s packs
Turned into tyrants, and the ancient world toppled
Into dreadful chaos, by many styled” climax”.
Transl. Christian Souchon 01.01.2006-2026 (c) (r) All rights reserved

After a description of the labourious construction of the European entity we find here a despising judgment of the new trends and developments in European art and culture during the nineties.
"Daphnis and Chloe" is the ancient and bucolic story of two foundlings brought up by goatkeepers on the island of Lesbos who gradually fell in love, their love developing from childlike innocence to full sexual maturity.
Caius Cornelius Gallus was considered by Ovid as the first elegiac poet.
