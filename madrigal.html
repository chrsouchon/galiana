X-2 MADRIGAL DE PRINTEMPS

Pour que l'éternité hantant votre printemps
Garde de son jardin la candeur qui s'achève,
Votre sein de la rose a recueilli la sève,
Votre lèvre le sang des oeillets éclatants.

Flots furent vos cheveux aux naufrages latents,
Caverne votre corps que l'appel peuple et grève 
Apothéose (Boboli somptueux) brève
Car la chair est l'humus où germent les instants.

Tandis qu'à son déclin votre chant s'achemine,
Je saurai déjouer l'appel de la vermine
Et fixer par des mots ce que fut votre avril.

Mon jardin ne connaît de terme ni d'absence.
Son rythme studieux gardera votre essence
Et le désir jamais n'y sera vain, ni vil.

Michel Galiana (c) 1990


X-2 MADRIGAL OF SPRING

So that eternity which haunted your spring
May preserve its garden’s freshness now on the wane
Your breast was apt to keep the rose’s sap living,
As have your lips the blood of the bright carnation.

Your hair was a tide where vessels would meet their fate
Your body a grotto longing people would fill
Brief apotheosis! (sumptuous Boboli Hill!),
For flesh is the humus where instants germinate.

While your subsiding song mov’s toward its decline,
I’ll know how to outwit decay’s venting its spleen 
And retain in my words what you were in your prime.

Unknown to my garden are limit and absence.
And its studious rhythm will preserve your essence,
And desire will be there neither futile nor mean.
Transl. Christian Souchon 01.01.2004 (c) (r) All rights reserved

Note :

En apparence, un charmant madrigal dans le style de Ronsard: la beauté féminine est assujétie au déclin. Mais le poète peut y remédier... Toutefois, étant donné la place du poème dans la série consacrée au Jardin des Hespéride, on peut douter qu'il ne s'agisse que de cela. Boboli est le jardin du palais Pitti de Florence.

Apparently, a charming madrigal in Ronsard's fashion: female beauty is subjected to decay. But the poet can help… But, considering that this poem is part of the series dedicated to the Garden of the Hesperides, it is doubtful that it be only that. The Boboli Hill is the garden of Palazzo Pitti in Florence.

"Madrigal de printemps is one of Galiana’s most symbolically concentrated poems: a meditation on ephemerality, eros, vegetal metaphysics, and the alchemical preservation of essence. The poem opens with a metaphysical paradox: eternity haunts spring. Spring is normally the season of beginnings, but here it is already shadowed by timelessness. The body becomes a garden-organ (breast, lip). These are not metaphors of sensuality alone; they are alchemical transmutations. Sap and blood are the vital fluids of vegetal and human life. The body receives them as if it were a matrix of preservation, a vessel capable of holding the fleeting.
Thus the beloved is not merely beautiful; she is the locus where nature’s essence is stored. This is the poem’s first metaphysical claim: The ephemeral seeks refuge in the living body.

The second quatrain shifts from the body-as-garden to the garden-as-body.

The cavern is a primordial symbol: womb, tomb, sanctuary. Desire fills it, then leaves it bare. This is the rhythm of eros: presence and absence, fullness and void.
