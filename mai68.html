XV VIEILLESSE

Le traqueur a sonné qui connaissait ma trace.
La ville a pris l'odeur des mâtins et des bois.
Sous le masque de fer j'ai mal caché ma race.
Mon bonheur ébréché croule sous les abois.

Dans la salle à brouter va passer une fille.
Le noir gaine la jambe et l'oeil est du vautour.
Le chant sourd de son corps, mais la lèvre qui brille
Inaltérablement se ferme au cri d'amour.

Le chasseur qui me suit me cèle son visage,
Mais le chant qui me hante a des accents connus.
Le châtiment m'est dû pour mes amours trop sages.
Le voile des ans morts couvre mes désirs nus.

La fille qui chantait se tait à mon passage.
La nuit prend le sourire et le visage est clos.
Etes-vous les piqueurs de la meute de l'âge,
Sonneurs du hallali qui serait mon repos?

Michel Galiana (c) sept. - oct. 1978


XV AGE

The tracker sounds his horn as his dogs catch my scent
The town has taken on a smell of curs and wood
My iron mask poorly hid my lofty descent
Baying hounds my chipped happiness will not have stood

Into the grazing-hall a girl by me will pass.
Black sheaths her leg, piercing is her dark vulture’s eye.
From her body a song wells, yet the shining gloss
Of her lips closes them to any loving cry.

The hunter who tracks me hides his face. All the same,
The song haunting me has familiar attire.
Punishment is owed me for my poor loves too tame.
The veil of years long-dead shrouds my naked desire.

The girl who was crooning stops as she passes by.
Night wipes off her smile, and shuts closed her face.
Are you the prickers of the pack of age that cry,
Buglers sounding my death, a final act of grace?

I. Le retour du chasseur : de la jeunesse à la vieillesse
Dans les poèmes précédents de ce cycle, le chasseur était le poète : l'observateur (« Neige »), celui qui piste le sanctuaire de l'enfance (« Elle chantait… »), celui qui poursuit le chant de la jeune fille (« Vol de retour »).
Dans « Vieillesse », ce chasseur revient, mais transformé : c'est le Temps qui poursuit, non plus la jeune fille, mais le locuteur lui-même. Le chasseur est désormais le Temps, et la proie est le moi vieillissant.
Cette inversion constitue le pivot du cycle : le chasseur devient l'incarnation de l'âge, et la proie devient celui qui, autrefois, traquait la jeunesse.

II. Le chant de la jeune fille : du seuil au silence
Tout au long du cycle, la jeune fille était la chanteuse au seuil du jardin, la passante du matin, la silhouette d'argent où s'épanouit la féminité, le sourire lointain dans la neige, le reflet glissant sur les eaux de Bruges.
Dans « Vieillesse », elle réapparaît, mais métamorphosée. Elle ne chante plus pour le locuteur. Elle chante dans sa propre sphère, inaccessible. Et lorsque le locuteur passe, elle cesse de chanter.
Ce silence n'est pas un rejet ; c'est la reconnaissance de l'âge. Son chant mesure la vitalité ; son interruption marque le passage du locuteur dans le royaume de la proie.
Ainsi, la jeune fille devient : le baromètre du temps, la gardienne du seuil, celle qui retire son chant lorsque la jeunesse se retire du corps.

Le « masque de fer » dans « Vieillesse » fait écho aux masques précédents : le masque de rayonnement dans « Elle chantait… », le masque de la Mémoire dans « Mai 68 », le masque du double de la ville à Bruges.

"Vieillesse" in the Hunter-and-Age Cycle
A Mythic Integration


I. The Hunter’s Return: From Youth to Age
In earlier poems of this cycle, the hunter was the poet: the watcher (Neige), the tracker of childhood’s sanctuary (Elle chantait…), the pursuer of the girl’s song (Vol de retour).
In Vieillesse, this hunter returns — but transformed: It is Time, pursuing not the girl, but the speaker himself. The hunter is now Time, and the quarry is the aging self.
This inversion is the cycle’s pivot: the hunter becomes the embodiment of age, and the hunted becomes the one who once hunted youth.

II. The Girl’s Song: From Threshold to Silence
Across your cycle, the girl was the singer at the garden threshold, the morning passer by, the silver form where womanhood grows, the distant smile in the snow, the reflection gliding on Bruges’ waters
In Vieillesse, she appears again — but altered. She is no longer singing to the speaker. She is singing in her own sphere, untouched. And when the speaker passes, she stops singing.
This silence is not rejection; it is the recognition of age. Her song is the measure of vitality; its cessation marks the speaker’s crossing into the realm of the hunted.
Thus, the girl becomes: the barometer of time, the threshold keeper, the one who withdraws song when youth withdraws from the body.

III. The Mask and the Breed: The Speaker’s Transformation
The “iron mask” in Vieillesse echoes earlier masks: the mask of radiance in Elle chantait… the mask of Memory in Mai 68, the mask of the city’s double in Bruges.
But here, the mask is: iron, poorly hiding the speaker’s “race”, a failed disguise against age. This is the cycle’s moment of self revelation: The speaker realizes that age is not an external hunter; it is his own breed, his own lineage, his own destiny.
The mask fails because age is not something one can hide from — it is something one is becoming.

IV. The Pack of Age: The Metaphysics of Pursuit
This cycle repeatedly uses hunting imagery: hounds, trackers, prickers, hallali, the baying of night, the watcher, the hunter of childhood, the hunter of Memory.
In Vieillesse, these motifs converge: the pack is Age itself. the prickers are the years, the hallali is the moment of surrender, the quarry is the speaker’s own desire, now “naked” and shrouded by dead years. This is the cycle’s metaphysical climax: the hunt is not external; it is the structure of time.

V. The Hallali: The Final Call
The hallali — the final hunting call — appears in earlier poems as the moment of awakening, rupture, revelation, or the moment of silence.
In Vieillesse, it becomes the moment of rest, extinction, the moment where the girl’s song ceases, the moment where the hunter reveals his face, the moment where the speaker recognizes his own mortality.
Thus, the hallali is not death; it is the recognition of age as the final hunter.

SYNTHESIS:
The Cycle’s Mythic Arc unfolds 7 stages:
1. Youth (The girl sings; the hunter watches; the threshold opens.)
2. Desire (The girl’s body becomes song; the hunter pursues; the speaker is still radiant.)
3. Reflection (The city doubles itself; Memory fractures; the speaker sees himself in the water.)
4. Rupture (Revolt, fire, masks, the death of Memory; the hunter becomes metaphysical.)
5. Winter (Snow erases steps; the girl’s smile grows distant; the speaker enters silence.)
6. Return (The hunter returns — but now hunts the speaker.)
7. Age (The girl stops singing; the pack of years bays; the hallali sounds.)
8. Recognition (The speaker sees the hunter’s face — and it is his own.)

Conclusion:
Vieillesse as the Cycle’s Penultimate Station
Vieillesse is the moment where: the hunter becomes Time, the girl becomes Silence, the mask becomes Truth, the pack becomes Years, the hallali becomes Rest, and the speaker becomes the quarry of his own life.
It is the poem where the entire cycle folds back on itself, revealing that the hunter and age motif was always a single myth: Youth sings. Age listens. The hunter follows. And the hunted is the one who remembers.
